I am a postdoctoral research associate in the Department of Computer Science at Princeton University, where I work with Prof. Andrés Monroy-Hernández in the DeCenter. I am also affiliated with: the Princeton HCI Lab; the Humans and Machines Lab led by Prof. Manoel Horta Ribeiro.

Prior, I was housed in the Center for Information Technology Policy (CITP), where I served as the Siegel Research Fellow (2024-26) and remain affiliated. I received my PhD at Northwestern University, advised by Prof. Aaron Shaw as part of the fantastic Community Data Science Collective.

My research explores how to support collective decision-making and ownership over social and AI technologies. I primarily publish in HCI/computing venues, but this work is deeply shaped by scholarship in the humanities and social sciences.

current projects

two core projects i am leading lately:

  • towards community-owned systems / concerns about data privacy and algorithmic fairness have long drawn calls to decentralize platforms away from large companies, but maintaining community-owned alternatives is laborious and lacks both technical and organizational support. building on my early work on how online communities adopt and develop governance practices, i collaborate with wonderful real-world partners like Bonfire and BlackSky to support communities in coordinating + building coalitions that govern together. own your systems!!
    • our vision paper: [CHI'26] from workshops we've run
      • (+ plugging some core related work at CSCW'24, '25, '26!)
    • governance cards, aka CROW cards: [site] [docs]
    • a theory piece (please, share comments!): [designing the seams]

  • (re)making the rules of the digital knowledge commons / wikimedia projects are critical to the digital knowledge commons - shaping downstream technologies - but face multiple threats to their survival. how do we make them more *robust*? i examine rules, processes, tools of wikis to understand how these projects might mitigate or exacerbate new challenges around information integrity and community engagement. some recent studies in this vein include:
    • rules and rule-making in the 5 largest wikis [ICWSM'22]
      • + expanded for other community scales, see: User:sohw
    • impact of deletion nominations in enwiki [osf]
    • evaluation of WikiVault (LLM translation tool) in kowiki [arXiv]
    • WIKI-PIE: Policy Invocation and Enforcement Dataset [osf]

i'm also involved in some projects with colleagues and students on more zeitgeist-y projects about novel technologies like AI and blockchain. i'm particularly interested in what comes ``after the hype:'' understanding how + why technologies become adopted by organizations in high-stakes contexts like humanitarian aid, what misalignments arise, and how we can learn from them. any info about those are probably on my `recent' stream. i try to keep it updated because i am bad with posting generally.
